## Fixed interpretation

- `Y=1`: weak and strong extracted answers disagree.
- `Y=0`: they agree.
- Action `1`: route to strong and observe `Y`.
- Action `0`: use weak and do not observe `Y`.
- Routing accuracy means agreement with the cached strong-model answer, not ARC
  ground-truth accuracy.

## Established experimental history

### Weak model and features

- The original Pythia-2.8B weak model was replaced by
  `Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` after experiments showed that Pythia answer
  quality was too low for useful disagreement routing.
- Tested context ideas included low-dimensional hidden-state PCA plus
  uncertainty, prompt-only embeddings, uncertainty-only contexts, and richer
  candidate features.
- The current cached context retains the two sources that were most useful in
  ablations: weak-model uncertainty and internal hidden states. It contains 14
  uncertainty features plus 64 fixed whitened-PCA features.
- The current PCA is fixed across rounds and may use all collected hidden states
  without labels. This is an explicit transductive, outcome-free choice.

### Current real-data player baseline

- ETC: XGBoost, 100 initial tastes, then freeze.
- CBPSide: regularized linear logistic model and confidence-based routing;
  class-balanced bootstrap of 10 agreements and 10 disagreements, capped at 50
  total tastes.
- IGW: XGBoost, fixed `gamma=32`, `mu=2`, no fixed minimum beyond the same
  class-balanced bootstrap, inverse-propensity weights capped at 10.
- Random routing is matched to ETC traffic.
- Default losses: `l01 = [1.82, 2.22, 2.67, 3.33]`, `l11 = 1`.

### Nonlinear synthetic sanity check

Data are generated independently with two features and

```text
P(Y=1|X) = sigmoid(x1*x2).
```

This deliberately violates a linear-logistic specification. Small HGB closely
approached the known Bayes oracle while linear logistic was near random,
confirming that the supervised and routing pipeline can expose strong nonlinear
structure when it is present. Current synthetic defaults are 3,000 supervised
training samples, 2,000 independent test/online samples, 500 ETC tastes, and 10
loss-derived thresholds spanning approximately `alpha=0.95` to `0.05`.

### Real-data residual diagnostic, 2026-09-02

Configuration:

- 2,351 eligible ARC-Easy examples;
- 78-dimensional cached context;
- five-fold stratified out-of-fold predictions;
- selected capacities: HGB-350 and MLP-8.

| Model | AUC | Log loss | Brier | 10-bin ECE | Significant residual bins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Linear logistic | 0.6646 | 0.6351 | 0.2228 | 0.0279 | 0/10 |
| HGB-350 | 0.6520 | 0.6436 | 0.2264 | 0.0289 | 3/10 |
| MLP-8 | 0.6415 | 0.6640 | 0.2330 | 0.0709 | 6/10 |

Interpretation: logistic was best calibrated and best on the main probability
metrics. HGB showed localized calibration errors and a compressed probability
range. MLP-8 was overconfident, underpredicting disagreement at low scores and
overpredicting it at high scores. Residual-versus-probability plots assess
calibration but do not establish the stronger condition `E[Y-p(X)|X]=0`.

Implemented next diagnostic: nested cross-fit an HGB residual regressor on the
original features and test whether it predicts held-out logistic residuals
better than a zero-residual baseline. For every outer fold, the evaluated rows
are absent from both the logistic and residual-learner fits; inner logistic
cross-fitting constructs the residual targets used for residual-learner
training. A shuffled-training-residual permutation reference tests whether the
observed held-out MSE improvement exceeds chance. Record the resulting metrics
below after the full-cache run.

### 2026-09-03 — Three-context holdout residual design

Branch: `experiment/residual-diagnostics`

Research question: compare the binned validation residuals of linear logistic
regression for (1) uncertainty plus internal-state PCA features, (2) prompt PCA
features, and (3) a controlled nonlinear prompt-feature target generated by a
shallow decision-tree teacher.

Design:

- cache: `llm-routing-cache-full.zip`, schema v2;
- 5,173 collected rows and 5,138 eligible rows according to the manifest;
- one stratified 80/20 train-validation split with seed 0;
- context boundaries discovered from `manifest.context_blocks`;
- scenario 1 combines the complete `uncertainty` and `hidden_state_pca` blocks;
- scenario 2 uses the complete `prompt_embedding_pca` block;
- scenario 3 uses a depth-two probabilistic decision-tree teacher split on the
  first three prompt components at their training medians, with fixed leaf
  probabilities `[0.10, 0.75, 0.30, 0.90]` and Bernoulli label seed 10000;
- real disagreement outcomes and validation information are not used to define
  or fit the synthetic teacher;
- all logistic models use training-split standardization and L2 regularization;
- validation raw residuals are `y - predicted_probability`;
- binned residuals use approximately equal-size bins ordered by predicted
  probability, with `round(sqrt(n_validation))` bins and 95% intervals.

Planned command:

```powershell
analyze-holdout-residuals `
  --cache llm-routing-cache-full.zip `
  --output-dir holdout-residual-results `
  --validation-fraction 0.2 `
  --seed 0
```

No numerical conclusions are recorded until the user runs this command and
returns the result bundle.

### 2026-09-03 — Three-context holdout residual result

Branch: `experiment/residual-diagnostics`

Configuration: schema-v2 `llm-routing-cache-full.zip`, 5,138 eligible rows,
seed 0, one stratified split with 4,110 training and 1,028 validation rows, and
32 approximately equal-size validation bins. The cached strong-model answer
remained the routing reference; ARC gold answers were not used.

| Logistic setting | Dimension | Validation AUC | Log loss | Brier | Accuracy | 95% intervals excluding zero |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Uncertainty + hidden state, real labels | 78 | 0.6260 | 0.6625 | 0.2328 | 0.6080 | 2/32 |
| Prompt features, real labels | 64 | 0.5799 | 0.6691 | 0.2383 | 0.5963 | 1/32 |
| Prompt features, tree-generated labels | 64 | 0.7494 | 0.5897 | 0.2010 | 0.6916 | 4/32 |

The uncertainty-plus-hidden context outperformed the prompt-only context on all
reported validation prediction metrics. Its two bin intervals excluding zero,
and the prompt model's one, are not persuasive evidence of misspecification
when 32 unadjusted intervals are inspected. The binned-residual-versus-fitted-
probability plot primarily assesses calibration and cannot establish the
stronger condition that residuals are unrelated to every input direction.

The controlled probabilistic tree used training-feature median splits on prompt
components 1--3 and leaf probabilities `[0.10, 0.75, 0.30, 0.90]`. The fitted
logistic student's validation mean residuals within those four generating
leaves were `[-0.2032, +0.2241, -0.2317, +0.1176]`. These large alternating
leaf errors expose the omitted tree interactions even though the global mean
residual was only `+0.0054`. The synthetic control therefore confirms that
conditioning residuals on relevant feature regions can reveal nonlinear
misspecification that an overall residual mean, or fitted-probability bins
alone, may obscure.

Decision: retain linear logistic regression as the parsimonious real-data
baseline. This holdout analysis does not provide strong binned-residual evidence
that either real context requires a nonlinear model, and prompt-only features
are clearly weaker than uncertainty plus hidden-state features in this split.
Use the synthetic leaf diagnostic as a positive control, not as evidence about
the real disagreement outcome.

Artifact location: `holdout-residual-results/holdout-residual-results.zip`.
